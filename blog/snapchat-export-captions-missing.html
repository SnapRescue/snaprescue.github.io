The problem

Snapchat export missing captions and stickers? Here is where they went

Updated November 2026 · about 4 min read

You open a photo from your Snapchat export and something is off. The picture is there, but the caption you typed across it is not. Neither is the sticker, the drawing, or the location tag. It looks like Snapchat threw them away.

It did not. They are in your export. They are just not on the picture any more.

Snapchat saves the caption as a second file

When you add text, a sticker or a drawing to a snap, Snapchat keeps two things: the plain photo or video, and a see-through image the same size that holds only what you added on top. In the app the two are shown together, so you never notice.

The export hands them to you separately. For each Memory that had anything on it, you get the plain file and a matching overlay file. In recent exports the pair looks like this:

  • 2021-08-14_..-main.jpg, the photo with nothing on it.
  • 2021-08-14_..-overlay.png, a transparent image with only the caption and stickers.

Older exports name the overlay differently, but the idea is the same. If you copy only the photos into your gallery, every caption stays behind in a file you never opened.

Why this matters more than it sounds

For a lot of Memories the caption is the point. It is the joke, the name of the place, the date you wrote on it, or who was there. A photo of a table with nothing written on it is not the same memory.

Putting them back by hand

For a photo you can do it in any image editor: open the plain file, put the overlay on top as a layer, and save. That is fine for five photos. It is not something anyone does for two thousand.

Videos are harder. The overlay has to be drawn over every frame and the video encoded again, which needs a video tool and some patience per clip.

Putting them back automatically

SnapRescue matches each overlay to its photo or video and merges them, so the saved copy looks the way it did in Snapchat. It does this for videos too, on the desktop app and on Android. If you would rather keep the clean version, you can turn the merge off and keep the plain files.

While it is there: the same pass writes the real date and location back into each file. Those are missing from the export as well, which is why your photo app thinks everything was taken today.

Check before you delete anything

Whatever tool you use, open a few Memories you remember having captions and make sure the text is back before you clear the export off your device. The overlay files are the only copy of that text outside Snapchat.
