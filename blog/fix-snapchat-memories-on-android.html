Fix your Snapchat Memories export on Android, no computer needed

Updated November 2026 · about 4 min read

You asked Snapchat for your Memories, the download landed on your phone, and now you have a folder of files that all claim to be from today. Almost every guide tells you to move them to a computer and fix them there. If your phone is the only device you use, that is where most people give up.

You do not need the computer. This is how to do the whole thing on an Android phone.

What you need

  • An Android phone running Android 8.0 or newer.
  • Your Snapchat data export, downloaded to the phone. If you have not requested it yet, this guide walks through it.
  • Enough free space for a second copy of your Memories. The fixed copies are saved alongside the originals, so nothing is overwritten.

Step 1: download your export to the phone

Snapchat emails you a link when your data is ready. Open it on the phone and download every file it lists. Depending on when you exported, you get either ZIP files or a folder of loose photos and videos with a json folder beside them. Both work.

Step 2: install SnapRescue for Android

SnapRescue for Android is a direct download from our site, not a Play Store app, so Android asks you to allow the install the first time. The install page has the download and the four taps it takes.

Step 3: point it at the export

Open the app and choose the folder your export is in. It reads Snapchat's memories_history file, matches every photo and video to its entry, and shows you what it found before it changes anything: how many Memories, how many have a location, and how many have captions.

Step 4: rescue

Tap Rescue. For each Memory the app writes the real capture date and GPS into the file, converts the time to the local time where it was taken, and puts captions and stickers back on, on videos as well as photos. You can lock the phone or switch apps while it runs. A big library takes a while, mostly because of the videos.

Where the fixed files go: into a SnapRescue album in your phone's gallery, already sorted by the day each one was taken. Google Photos picks them up from there like any other album.

Step 5: look through them

The app has its own viewer for what it rescued: a timeline by date, an On this day view, and a map with a pin for every Memory that had a location. It is a quick way to check the dates came out right before you delete anything.

What it costs

The first 100 files are free, so you can see the result on your own Memories. Unlocking the rest is a one-time $9.99. You buy a licence on the site and type the key into the app. There is no subscription and no account.

Is it private?

Everything happens on the phone. Your photos and videos are not uploaded anywhere. The app goes online for two things: checking your licence key, and an optional lookup that turns GPS coordinates into place names.
