The problem

Why your Snapchat Memories show the wrong time after export

Updated November 2026 · about 4 min read

You fixed the dates on your Snapchat export, or a tool did it for you, and the photos are finally on the right day. Then you look closer. Dinner happened at ten in the morning. A sunrise is filed under the night before. Some photos have slipped onto the day after.

Nothing is broken. The times are correct. They are just not in your time zone.

Snapchat records every Memory in UTC

The list of Memories inside your export gives each one a date and time in UTC, the reference time zone that computers use. It does not record what the clock said where you were standing.

If you live in London in winter, UTC and local time are the same and you never notice. Anywhere else, every time is out by your distance from UTC.

A real example

One Memory in our test library is recorded as 2023-01-14 10:00:05 UTC and was taken in Newtown, Sydney. Sydney is 11 hours ahead of UTC in summer, so the real moment was 9:00 PM. Copy the UTC time across as it is and a night out is stamped 10 AM.

The same gap moves the date. Anything taken in Sydney before 11 AM is still the previous day in UTC, so a breakfast photo lands on yesterday.

Why a single fixed offset does not fix it

The quick fix is to add your time zone's offset to everything. That goes wrong in two ways:

  • Travel. Photos from a trip get your home offset, not the offset of where you were.
  • Daylight saving. The offset changes during the year, so half your library ends up an hour out.

What does fix it

The export also records where most Memories were taken. From the location you can work out the time zone, and from the time zone and the date you can work out the exact offset on that day, daylight saving included.

That is what SnapRescue does. For each Memory it looks up the time zone from the GPS position and writes the real local time into the file. A photo taken at 7 PM on holiday is saved as 7 PM, wherever home is.

One limit: a Memory with no location has nothing to look up. For those, SnapRescue uses the time zone of the device you run it on. If you took them at home, that is right. If you took them abroad with location off, they will be out by the difference.

How to check yours

Pick a Memory where you know roughly when it happened, such as a sunset or a birthday dinner, and look at the time your photo app shows. If it is out by a whole number of hours, you are looking at UTC.
