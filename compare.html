Snapchat Memories export tools compared

Checked 11 November 2026 · about 6 min read

Snapchat's export hands your Memories back with no dates, no locations and the captions in separate files. Plenty of tools now fix this, and they differ in ways that matter: what they run on, what they cost, and whether your photos have to be uploaded to someone else's server.

We make one of them, so read this knowing that. To keep it fair, every detail about another tool comes from that tool's own website on the date above, and where a site does not say something, the table says "not stated" and we do not guess.

The short version

  • Only have an iPhone? SnapRescue does not run on iPhone yet. Memport, Still Mine and FixMyExport do.
  • Want nothing uploaded? Pick a tool that runs on your own device. SnapRescue, FixMyExport, GetMemoriesOut, Memport and Still Mine all work that way. SnapEasy and MemoriesExport each offer an upload route and a local route.
  • Long videos cut into 10 second clips? SnapRescue does not join them. FixMyExport, SnapEasy and GetMemoriesOut say they do.
  • Using Immich or another self-hosted library, or care about the exact local time? That is where SnapRescue is strongest. Details below.

Notes on each tool

FixMyExport (formerly ExportSnaps)

ExportSnaps renamed itself FixMyExport. It has the widest device coverage here, with apps for Windows, Mac, iPhone, iPad and Android, and it processes on your device. The first 200 files are free. The paid version is a single payment, and sending the results to your own cloud storage is a separate add-on listed at $7.99. Its feature list includes joining split videos.

SnapEasy

SnapEasy gives you two routes for one price. The website takes an upload of your export, processes it on their servers and deletes the upload after 48 hours. The desktop app for Windows and Mac does the same job on your own computer. It lists $14.99 as an early price and says long videos are rejoined. It can send the finished archive to Google Drive.

MemoriesExport

MemoriesExport offers three routes: an online service that deletes your files an hour after you download the result, a mode that runs inside your browser without uploading, and a desktop app. The first 100 Memories are free and the full version is $9.99.

GetMemoriesOut

GetMemoriesOut runs in a browser on a computer and does not upload your photos. It has the largest free allowance in this list, 500 Memories, and sorts into year and month folders. Its site is clear about its limits: it does not work on phones yet, and videos are only joined when the order of the clips is certain.

Memport

Memport is an iPhone app. You drop the ZIPs into it and it saves your Memories into Photos with their dates and places, all on the phone. It is free to try and $9.99 to unlock everything.

Still Mine

Still Mine is a phone app for iPhone and Android from a studio in Belgium. Browsing your archive is free. Saving everything to your gallery is a single €4.99 payment per archive, the lowest price here.

SnapSavior

SnapSavior is an upload service that keeps your files for up to 7 days. When we checked, its site carried a notice that the service is paused, so confirm it is running before you rely on it.

Where SnapRescue fits

SnapRescue is a desktop and Android app. It never uploads your photos and does not need an account. The first 100 files are free with every feature switched on, and $9.99 once unlocks the rest on up to 3 devices, with a 30 day refund.

Three things it does that are worth checking for in any tool you consider:

  • Real local time. Snapchat records times in UTC. SnapRescue works out the time zone from each Memory's location and writes the time it was where you were standing, daylight saving included. Why that matters.
  • Place names. It turns coordinates into readable places, so you can see where a photo was taken without opening a map.
  • XMP sidecars. For Immich external libraries and similar setups, it can write the metadata into a sidecar file next to each original. The Immich guide covers this.

And two things it does not do: it has no iPhone version yet, and it leaves videos that Snapchat split into 10 second clips as separate clips.

How to choose

Almost every tool here lets you try before paying. Use that. Run your real export through the free allowance and check a few photos you know well: the day, the time of day, the place, and whether the caption is back on. A tool that gets those right on 100 files will get them right on 10,000.
